AUCKLAND ON A COPROCESSOR CARD - the protocol
=============================================

The card (a Raspberry Pi Pico on the CPC's bus, like the CPC-CPLINK2 or the M4) keeps
AUCKLAND.DAT and does the 3D: the camera, the tiles, culling, transforming, clipping and
projecting. The CPC sends its keys and draws the lines the card hands back - it stays the
computer that runs the demo, with its own line drawing; the card is its maths and its
storage. No RAM expansion needed on the CPC.

The card serves a ROM (as the M4 does): what the card writes into its 16K, the CPC reads at
#C000-#FFFF with that ROM selected (KL ROM SELECT / KL ROM RESTORE, or OUT #DFxx and the
upper ROM enabled). Commands go the other way through the card's I/O port.


THE CARD'S ROM (16K, the card writes, the CPC reads)
----------------------------------------------------
#C000-#C0FF   the card's own ROM header (whatever its firmware needs)
#C100         the status block, 48 bytes:
  +0  "S3DP"          the card is there and has the data
  +4  seq             +1 each time a new list is complete (written last)
  +5  which list      0: #C200, 1: #E000 - the newest
  +6  lines (word)    lines in it
  +8  altitude (word) metres
  +10 compass dx      -15..15: the way to the city's centre on the screen, from above
  +11 compass dy      (dx right, dy down: up the screen is ahead)
  +12 views           how many (1-9 on the keys)
  +16 message         24 characters, 0 padded: a view's name
#C200-#DFFF   draw list 0 (7680 bytes)
#E000-#FDFF   draw list 1 (7680 bytes)
The card makes each new list in the one the CPC didn't just get, so the CPC can copy one
out while the card fills the other.


A DRAW LIST (3 bytes a point, mode 1's 320 x 200, y 0 at the top)
-----------------------------------------------------------------
  00 0000 0x  xxxxxxxx  yyyyyyyy    MOVE: the pen to (x, y)  (x 0-319: 9 bits)
  01 0000 0x  xxxxxxxx  yyyyyyyy    DRAW: a line from the pen to (x, y), the pen there
  10 0000 pp                        PEN p (1 byte): 1 white, 2 green, 3 cyan
  11 000000                         END (1 byte)
Every point is on the screen already (clipped by the card). Lines that join share their
points (a building's top is one MOVE and n DRAWs): about 1.8 points a line, 5.5 bytes.
The CPC side's loop (src/bas/aucklink/Link.cls, Draw): about 25 Z80 instructions a point,
then the library's gfx_line.


COMMANDS (CPC to card: a packet - the bytes out through the card's port, then a kick)
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  'H'                                  hello: the status block filled in ("S3DP", views)
  'V' n                                the camera at view n (0-8); its name in the message
  'N' n                                view n's name in the message
  'F' pitch roll yaw speed skip budget the frame: pitch, roll, yaw -1 / 0 / 1 (signed bytes,
                                       2 degrees each), speed (units of 0.5 m a frame), skip
                                       (0-8: moved 1 + skip times), budget (word: lines at
                                       most). The card moves the camera, makes the next list,
                                       then seq + 1
The stand-in ports in Link.cls (CMDP #FE00 for the bytes, KICKP #FC00 for the kick) are the
M4's way: change them to the CPC-CPLINK2's.


A FRAME ON THE CPC
------------------
  1 wait until seq changes (read #C104)            - the card's list is ready
  2 copy the list out (LDIR from #C200 / #E000)    - 7.5K at most: about 45 ms
  3 send 'F' with the keys                         - the card makes the next one now
  4 clear the screen being drawn on, draw the list, the compass; Flip
So the card works while the CPC draws: a frame costs the CPC its copy and its drawing only.


WHAT IT GIVES (measured)
------------------------
The card's side (tools/s3dpi, s3dpi.c - integer only, no FPU): 0.03-0.2 ms a frame on a PC;
on a 133 MHz RP2040, roughly 100 times slower, still a few ms - nothing next to the CPC.
The CPC's side, drawing lists the card made (the Z80 drawing, in the NTC harness):
  start view, 440 lines, 1.7K       0.29 s a frame   (3.4 frames a second)
  Harbour Bridge, 300 lines, 1.3K   0.22 s           (4.5)
  Devonport, 1270 lines, 5.6K       0.86 s           (1.2)
  Sky Tower, 1400 lines, 7.7K       1.13 s           (0.9)
Against the Z80 doing it all: 2.5-4.5 s a frame (0.2-0.4 frames a second). So about 4 to 10
times faster - and now drawing the lines is nearly all the CPC's time: the next thing to
speed up is gfx_line itself (a mode 1 line routine with octant-specific loops, or the card
handing back runs of bytes), and a lower line budget (B) trades detail for speed.


WHERE THE DATA LIVES
--------------------
- on the card: AUCKLAND.DAT (3.8 MB) in its flash, read in place (s3d_open takes a pointer:
  on an RP2040 the flash is mapped at 0x10000000) - a board with 4 MB of flash or more
- or at the other end of the link (a PC or a Pi), the engine running there: the card then
  passes the 'F' packets up and the lists (7.5K a frame) down into its ROM


THE CODE (tools/s3dpi)
----------------------
  s3dpi.h / s3dpi.c   the engine: s3d_open, s3d_view, s3d_steer, s3d_frame, s3d_compass
  card.h / card.c     the card's side: card_init (the data), card_command (one packet); it
                      writes the status block and the lists into k->rom[] - the 16K the
                      card's firmware serves as the ROM
  sim.c               the link simulated in one program: packets in, the status watched,
                      the list copied out and drawn as the Z80 does (PPM pictures, and the
                      first list as a file for the CPC side's test)
  host.c              the engine alone: frames, their lists' sizes and times, pictures
Build: gcc -O2 sim.c card.c s3dpi.c -o sim   (any C compiler; the Pico SDK for the card:
card.c + s3dpi.c, with the firmware calling card_command for each packet)
  sim AUCKLAND.DAT 2 4 out     view 3 (the Sky Tower), 4 frames: out000.ppm ..., out.LST
CPC side: src/bas/aucklink (Main.bas, Link.cls - NTC); with no card it draws FRAME.LST.


THE CPC-CPLINK2 INTERFACE (CPC SIDE)
------------------------------------
This is what Link.cls talks to on a real CPC-CPLINK2 (github.com/rabs664/Amstrad-CPC-CPLink2).
The sim/card C above is protocol-agnostic (it fills a 16K ROM image); these are the registers
the CPC side uses to reach it. Two channels: a byte FIFO for commands out, the served ROM for
the status block and lists in.

IO registers
  #FD80  DATA     the FIFO. Write a byte -> the PI (CPC FIFO); read a byte <- the PI (PI FIFO).
  #FD81  STATUS   read: bit0 DOR (a byte is ready to read), bit1 DIR (the FIFO can take a byte),
                  bit2 ROR1, bit3 ROR2 (a ROM has new data), bits4-7 fixed 1010 (so a present
                  card reads as #Ax - a card-detect). Write any value = RESET: FIFOs and ROM
                  write counters to 0, DIR high, DOR low.
  #DFxx  ROMSEL   write 32 -> select PI ROM 1, 33 -> PI ROM 2 (the standard upper-ROM select,
                  i.e. KL ROM SELECT); any other value de-selects (and clears ROR1/ROR2).

The served ROMs
  PI ROM 1 and 2 are upper ROMs 32 and 33, each 16K at #C000-#FFFF (the card drives ROMDIS to
  hide the internal ROM when selected; a LOW on ROMEN + A14 high marks the upper-ROM read).
  The two act as one 32K ROM, written in 16K halves. The s3dpi 16K layout (header, status block
  at #C100, lists at #C200 / #E000) lives in PI ROM 1, so Link.cls reads with ROM 32 selected.
  After selecting, allow the PI a settle time before the read (spec: TBD).

Sending a command (Link.Send)      Reading a frame (Link.Wait / Fetch)
  for each byte of the packet:       KL ROM SELECT 32
    wait #FD81 bit1 (DIR) high        LDIR the status block from #C100, the newest list from
    OUT #FD80, byte                   #C200 or #E000 (status +5 says which)
  (the FIFO frames the stream -       KL ROM RESTORE
   no kick byte, unlike the M4)       the new list is ready when status +4 (seq) changes

At startup: KL ROM SELECT the PI ROM, write #FD81 once to reset, then Hello (the status block
reads "S3DP"). With no card the ROM read misses, Hello fails, and the CPC draws FRAME.LST.
